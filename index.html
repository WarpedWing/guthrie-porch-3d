PBATT help

Getting around

Drag to orbit, right-drag or two-finger drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. The model is to scale: its scale is set by the tile spacing (tile plus grout), 12.2 in by default; the Tile spacing slider adjusts it, and every inch reading updates.

In moonlight, the cut-away roof and arch still cast their shadows, so the moonlit edge stays accurate. In daylight with the cutaway on, the sun is placed overhead so the whole floor is evenly lit for close inspection.

Measure & path tools

  • Measure: tap two points. Drag an end to adjust it.
  • Wheel path: tap to add points, then drag a point, or drag the line itself to bend it. New path starts another path in a new colour. Tire width sets the band width.
  • Second wheel: shows where the other wheel on the same axle would run, on the left or right of the active path as seen walking from its first point. Wheel spacing sets the distance between the wheel centres. Wherever the drawn path bends more tightly than the wheel spacing (a radius under, say, 16″ for a 16″ axle), the other wheel must briefly roll backward; that stretch is amber. This is geometry, not an error: a hand truck does it whenever it turns that sharply. Pale yellow is the sweep at a pivot point. Hand-placed bends are often sharper than the real route, so smoothing a bend usually clears the amber. Red means that wheel would hit a wall, pillar or bed.
  • Pivot point (shown when a pivot is selected) puts the turning point anywhere along the axle line, in inches from wheel 1 toward wheel 2 (0 = on wheel 1, the wheel spacing = on wheel 2, negative = outside wheel 1). Small values make wheel 1 run a tight arc of that radius: it twists more than it rolls, so its tread smears.
  • Tilt here (on a selected point) cycles tip back (violet ring), set down (grey ring) or no change: from that point the playback chair tilts onto its rear wheels or back onto all four, easing over about 4″ of travel. Start tipped back sets how every path begins. Caster paths are drawn only where the chair is level, since tipped back the casters are off the floor. Load is the total weight (occupant plus chair, 170 lb by default). Each path's Details gives a rough one-person effort: the push to keep it rolling, the force at the handles for each pivot, and the much larger force a sideways drag would need. The numbers assume rubber on tile (friction 0.7, rolling resistance 0.02) and are estimates.
  • Pivot and Reverse: tap a path point (a white ring shows it's selected). Pivot cycles how the truck turns there: on wheel 1 (the wheel whose path you drew stays put, amber ring), on wheel 2 (the other wheel stays put and wheel 1 swings round it, orange ring), or spin (the truck twists about the middle of its axle and the wheels roll opposite ways, purple ring). Reverse (cyan ring) makes the truck back up from that point without turning: draw the next points back the way it came. Each wheel's stamps count its actual rolling, so they run backward while it rolls backward and a patch can print twice. Pivots on wheel 2 and spins need a wheel spacing and side, so set Second wheel first (it assumes left if off). Paler stretches are pivots; amber means that wheel is rolling backward.
  • Second wheel stamps: with Second wheel and Wheel stamps both on, the other wheel gets its own stamps in pink, counted from the distance it actually rolls. Where it rolls backward the count unwinds (shown with −), so it can print the same patch twice.
  • Model switches playback between bare wheels and the stair chair. Choosing it sets Tire width to 2″, Wheel diameter to 6″ and Wheel spacing to the chair's Rear track (19″), so the tracks, stamps and Second wheel match the chair (switching back restores your settings). Wheel 1 (the drawn path) is one rear wheel and the other sits Wheel spacing away on the second-wheel side. Caster paths draws where the 6251's front casters roll for each path (green), using the chair's Wheelbase. The chair rolls with its rear wheels on the path (with Second wheel on, its rear track takes the Wheel spacing), faces the way the truck faces, and its front casters swivel like real trailing casters, so you can see them swing round when the chair reverses or pivots.
  • Play rolls a wheel (two with Second wheel on) along the active path at about 2 ft/s, at the set diameter and tire width; the red mark on the tire shows it turning, including backward on reverses and pivots. Drag Position to scrub.
  • Path details (segments, tiles, stamps and so on) are folded under each path's Details.
  • Stamp zero slides the 0 mark (and every stamp with it) forward or back along the path by up to one full tire turn, for when the start point isn't where the tire's mark first touched.
  • Wheel stamps: 0 marks the start, and reappears if the wheel rolls back past it. Stamps mark where a tire of the set Wheel diameter touches the floor as it rolls along the active path, starting from its first point (put that where the wheel stood, under the axle). Half turns (½, 1½, …) show where a spot that started on top of the tire lands; whole turns (1, 2, …) show where the spot that started on the floor lands again.
  • Blood path: a thin line for tracing blood trails, with a dot at the start and an arrow at the end. Tap open ground twice to draw a new line (first tap is the start, second the end). Tap or drag the line itself to add a bend; drag a point to move it; double-tap a point to delete it. Shift-tap extends the current line instead of starting a new one.
  • Arrows along wheel tracks show which way the truck faces: white when that wheel is rolling forward, amber when it's rolling backward.
  • Show buttons hide or show each layer. Save downloads a session file (all paths, pins, measurements and settings); Load restores one.
  • Sliders: use the ‹ › buttons (hold to repeat), the mouse wheel, or the arrow keys for single steps; click the value to type an exact number.
  • Mark imprint: tap each imprint to drop a numbered pin, then Copy imprint list to copy their positions.
  • Editing: double-tap any point or pin to delete it; a measure end deletes the whole measurement. Undo point removes the last thing you added.

Spatter source

A point that sprays in straight lines, shown as a red light. Floor it can reach turns red; anything in the way (the chair's footplate, the figure, walls) leaves its shadow, with holes letting spray through. Real drops curve down under gravity, so treat the shadow as close to the source and approximate further out. Tap floor to place puts the source above the tapped spot; Height lifts it; Reach limits how far the red spreads; Dim other lights darkens the scene so the shadow stands out. Spread narrows the spray from all directions to a cone, aimed with Direction (0° toward the street, 90° toward column 1) and Aim (below 0° points down). Scatter fills the spray volume with faint red specks so its shape shows in 3D (the specks ignore shadows; the floor colouring is the accurate part). Drag the chair to move the shadow.

Stair chair

An approximate Stryker Stair-PRO evacuation chair. The 6251 has a foot support between its front casters: a flat centre with three holes in front and two behind, and a wing each side angling up to the leg with two holes each; Footplate holes on floor drops each hole straight down (cyan rings). The 6250's front legs pinch in slightly to a floor bar. Its rear wheels are the 6×2″ wheel 6060-002-010 (also used on Stryker cots); each can be set to the Stryker OEM tread or the aftermarket replacement tread (24 wedges per side). The 6250 has front legs joined by a bar that rests on the floor, and is rolled tipped back on its two rear wheels; level, the bar's contact shows as an amber strip; the 6251 adds small front swivel casters so it can also roll on all four. The 16.4″ wheelbase (rear axle to front leg) is measured from Stryker's side-view drawing, scaled by the 6″ wheel (it matches the 28″ published depth). The rear track (about 19″, centre to centre) is estimated from the 20.5″ frame width. Tip back lifts the front off the floor about the rear axle. Drag it to move, Shift-drag to turn. Lock in place pins it and lets taps pass through it, so you can mark or draw underneath. Left/Right wheel choose each rear wheel's tread.

What's estimated

Ceiling height, pillar overhang, doorbell camera position (fitted to the footage), the left gravel bed and plants, wreath, mailer and front planting. Tiles not visible in any image are shown as plain tiles. Everything outside the porch is approximate.
